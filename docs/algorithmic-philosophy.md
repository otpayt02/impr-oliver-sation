# Signal Becomes Song

The piece begins with a simple proposition: hearing is a kind of computation, but musical understanding is not reducible to a machine readout. Binary is used as the raw condition—small, repeated decisions without feeling. The field begins legible and disciplined on the left, then loses its rectangular certainty as it crosses the screen.

Scroll is the performer. Nothing important happens merely because time passes; the viewer supplies the phrasing by moving. The transformation is therefore reversible and inspectable. Move forward and the data organizes into notation. Move back and the notation remembers its source.

The middle is transcription, not decoration. Five staff lines briefly give the particles a shared grammar, while each seeded element resolves toward a note position. This moment represents AP Music & Audio's central skill: hearing an ambiguous sound and finding the playable structure inside it.

The right side rejects a perfectly typeset ending. Notes dissolve into brick, eucalyptus, dusty periwinkle, mushroom, and mineral-grey grains. The result is useful music, but still alive—performance, recording, teaching, sampling, or a new software instrument rather than a fixed genre label.

The system is deterministic so the same seed tells the same story on every visit. Small oscillations and pointer reactions add breath without changing the composition. Motion controls and a complete static state make the work available without requiring animation, speed, or sound.
