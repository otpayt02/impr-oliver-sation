# Improvisation Analysis Pipeline — Phase 1

Purpose: turn one owner-provided improvisation into a reviewable map of form, harmony, pulse, hand roles, and moments of rhythmic departure. This is an analysis aid, not an automatic verdict about artistic intent.

## Input contract

- One original or owner-cleared piano performance video or stereo WAV, plus the session tempo if known.
- If possible: a locked camera that shows both hands and the keyboard; a separate clean stereo audio file; optional MIDI export. MIDI is the only reliable source for precise pitch, note onset, duration, and hand separation.
- Keep the original unedited file and record date. Covers, commercial backing tracks, and unlicensed reference audio stay local until rights are decided.

## Deliverable: the improv map

| Lane | What is marked | Evidence method | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Form | entry, development, contrast, peak, return, ending | timestamped listening notes | Where does the piece change its job? |
| Pulse | governing beat, meter, tempo anchors, return-to-one points | click reference and onset grid | Can a listener still locate the shared beat? |
| Rhythm | subdivision, displacement, syncopation, polyrhythmic tension | audio/MIDI onset comparison | Is the tension intentional, and where does it resolve? |
| Harmony | bass/root motion, voicing families, chord hypotheses, cadence | MIDI first; ear-checked chord map otherwise | What can be named confidently and what remains a hypothesis? |
| Hands | left-hand time/harmony role; right-hand melody/rhythm role; role swaps | video plus MIDI if available | Which hand is carrying the band-facing time cue? |
| Energy | density, register, dynamics, silence, articulation | timestamped listening notes | Where does experimental language begin and why? |

## Capture and analysis sequence

1. Record 5–8 minutes with a spoken slate: date, piece name, planned pulse, and whether a click is audible only in headphones.
2. Export the untouched master, a clean audio file, and MIDI if the instrument supports it. Do not manufacture stems from a stereo piano performance; label mono/stereo capture honestly.
3. Make a first listening pass without pausing: write only timestamps for entrances, energy turns, and obvious returns to the beat.
4. Add the pulse grid. Mark the steady internal metronome, then separately mark intentional apparent slowdown, displacement, or regrouping.
5. Mark harmony as certainty levels: **heard root**, **probable chord quality**, **voicing color**, or **unresolved/ambiguous**. Avoid claiming a chord progression where the performance does not support one.
6. Add hands from the video/MIDI. Note the practical band signal: repeated interval figures, bass anchors, comping attacks, or an exposed right-hand lead.
7. Review the result once with no click and once with the click. The goal is not to prove complexity; it is to show whether tension resolves back to a shared pulse.

## The persistent-pulse exercise

Start with an unambiguous 4/4 groove, a simple left-hand time cue, and clear downbeats. Keep one internal beat constant. Let a phrase deliberately appear to stretch, arrive late, or regroup while the internal beat does not move. Return to a clean downbeat before adding another disruption.

For a band setting, the piano has a responsibility: experimentation cannot cost the drummer the form. Keep a visible or audible anchor in the left hand, bass motion, repeated interval, or comping placement when the right hand gets freer. If the drummer loses the shared pulse because the piano obscured it, treat that as a performance note to solve, not proof that the idea was advanced.

## What Phase 1 can and cannot analyze

- **Can:** create an annotated session map, chord hypotheses, tempo/pulse observations, hand-role notes, and a learning-focused before/after edit.
- **Cannot yet:** reliably infer exact left/right hands from ordinary audio alone; split true stems from a single piano recording; judge “avant-garde” objectively; or promise an AI product without a defined data, privacy, accuracy, and review contract.

## First three portfolio captures

1. **The persistent-pulse return** — original 60–90 second performance that begins plainly, displaces a phrase, then lands cleanly back on one.
2. **Hear it, map it, play it** — an owner-cleared/original short phrase; show root finding, chord spacing, and a playable response. Do not use recognizable commercial audio publicly until rights are cleared.
3. **One piano, three roles** — left hand as time/harmony, right hand as lead, then a short explanation of how the piano supports the drummer.
